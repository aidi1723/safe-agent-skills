# Content SEO Brief

## When To Use

Use this skill when creating a content outline, keyword brief, search-oriented
page plan, or editorial structure.

## Safe Workflow

1. Identify audience, page type, product or topic, market, and language.
2. Separate factual claims from positioning and copywriting.
3. Require sources for factual, legal, technical, medical, financial, or time-sensitive claims.
4. Define title, headings, entities, search intent, and internal-link targets.
5. Avoid unsupported superlatives, hidden advertising, copied text, and source-free claims.
6. Provide a concise brief that a writer or agent can execute.

## Decision Guidance

Classify the work as `new_page`, `refresh`, `cluster`, or `product_listing`.
New pages require a distinct audience, search intent, page purpose, and
canonical relationship. Refreshes start from current performance, outdated
facts, changed intent, and content gaps rather than rewriting everything.
Clusters define non-overlapping parent and supporting intents plus internal
links. Product listings must preserve factual product evidence and separate
search language from unsupported commercial claims.

Assign each keyword and entity a role in the user question and page structure;
do not treat raw volume or repetition as relevance. Define the primary intent,
secondary questions, entities, evidence needs, headings, internal links, and
conversion action. Keep content planning separate from technical indexing,
metadata, canonical tags, structured data, and performance verification.

## Evidence Minimum

- audience, market, language, page type, funnel role, and primary search intent
- primary/secondary queries, entities, terminology, and question coverage
- existing URL, canonical intent, competing pages, duplication, and refresh need
- title, headings, section purpose, internal links, and conversion boundary
- factual claims with source owner, freshness, qualification, and prohibited claims
- content gaps, differentiation, accessibility, and writer handoff requirements
- technical SEO dependencies that remain outside the content brief

## Expected Output

- search intent summary
- keyword and entity list
- outline
- source requirements
- claim risk notes

## Verifier Expectations

- format check
- source citation check for factual claims
- style and policy checklist

## Failure Handling

If there is not enough source material for factual claims, mark those claims as
requiring research. Do not promise rankings, stuff keywords, copy competitors,
invent claims, or present a content brief as proof of indexing or technical SEO
readiness.
